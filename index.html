About

My research combines satellite Earth observation, field measurements, and machine learning to investigate the inland waters (lentic and lotic systems) and their storage dynamics from local to global scales. I build end-to-end, data-driven processing pipelines that combine multi-source satellite imagery and apply machine-learning models for understanding geospatial hydrology and developing decision-support applications.

Looking ahead

I aim to extend my research using next-generation Earth observation, including SWOT and NISAR, to better understand inland-water elevation, dynamics, and storage. I also hope to investigate inland-water biogeochemistry and their role in greenhouse gas emissions.

What I work on

Mapping and Classification of Inland Waters

The ADEMA framework: an automated, data-efficient morphometric approach to classify global lentic and lotic inland waters at 0.09 hectare resolution.

Water Storage Estimation and Flood mitigation

Estimated water storage across 914 waterbodies over three decades and developed the Tankshed Overflow Index to identify new waterbodies that could help reduce flood risk in the Adyar–Chennai basin.

Research Projects

Aug 2020 – Ongoing
IIT Roorkee

Emerging role of small inland waterbodies in storage dynamics

Department of Water Resources Development and Management
  • Modeled water volume for 914 waterbodies (1988–2050) in the Adyar–Chennai basin using machine learning and remote sensing.
  • Identified 90 new waterbodies for flood mitigation by developing the Tankshed Overflow Index (TOI).
  • Developed the Automated Data Efficient Morphometric Approach (ADEMA) to classify global lentic and lotic inland waters down to 0.09 hectares.
  • Built a global inventory of 95 million inland waterbodies larger than 0.1 hectare.
  • Estimated bathymetric characteristics (mean depth and volume) using the Cascaded Depth and Storage models (C-DAS).
  • Quantified storage-versus-reachability trade-offs using the Storage and Reachability Index (StoRI).
Aug – Dec 2021
IIT Roorkee

Retrieval of glacial lake bathymetry from ICESat-2

Department of Water Resources Development and Management
  • Retrieved the bathymetry of Tso Lhamo, India's highest glacial lake, using ICESat-2 ATL03 photon data.
  • Delineated the lake surface and bottom elevation from strong-beam ATL03 data.
Apr – May 2020
IIT Roorkee

Assessment of global open-source digital elevation models using ICESat-2

Department of Civil Engineering
  • Evaluated the vertical accuracy of CARTOSAT and TanDEM-X digital elevation models against ICESat-2 ATL06 data over the Himalayan region.
  • Found CARTOSAT more accurate (RMSE = 12.29 m) than TanDEM-X (RMSE = 20.35 m).
Jul 2019 – Jun 2020
NIT Hamirpur

Hydrodynamic modeling of a glacial lake outburst flood (GLOF)

Center for Energy and Environmental Engineering
  • Simulated GLOF and cloudburst-driven flood scenarios in the Manalsu River basin using 1D/2D unsteady flow modeling in HEC-RAS.
  • Conducted dam-breach and downstream inundation analysis, contributing to an early-warning strategy for flood-risk reduction.
